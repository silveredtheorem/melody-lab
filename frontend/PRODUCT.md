# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Small bands and music collectives — 2 to 6 musicians writing, arranging, and producing together asynchronously. They're not professional studio engineers; they're semi-pro and indie creators who care about their craft, work across time zones and schedules, and want their tools to feel as considered as the music they make.

## Product Purpose

Melody Lab is GitHub for collaborative music production. It gives bands version control for audio: branches, commits, merge conflicts, layer-by-layer diffs. The core job is to let a group of musicians work on the same project without stepping on each other — and to have a real history of how the song evolved.

Success looks like: a band can work on a track from three cities, merge their changes, resolve a conflict between two guitar takes, and ship a version — all without emailing stems around.

## Brand Personality

Creative · Playful · Expressive — but with precision underneath.

"Cute, handmade, looks like effort was put into it." Every surface should feel like a human made a deliberate choice, not like a template was applied. Bubbly but not juvenile. Polished but not corporate. Lowercase, but not sloppy.

References that capture the feel: monkeytype (typographic care, satisfying micro-interactions, cult following), linear.app (speed and polish, every detail earned), fly.io (confident copy, developer-trusted, nothing wasted).

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS scaffold** — Notion-clone IA, empty-state illustrations, "Welcome to your workspace" copy, identical card grids. Even if the visual polish is high (like Linear), the structural patterns shouldn't feel borrowed.
- **DAW skeuomorphism** — no knobs, rack units, fake wood, VU meters, or anything trying to look like hardware.
- **Streaming-app consumer mode** — Spotify/Apple Music energy. This is a tool for makers, not a listening experience.
- **Web3 / crypto dark + neon** — overdone and untrustworthy. The purple accent is the brand's own, not a DeFi tell.

## Design Principles

1. **Precision as personality.** Bubbly and polished are not opposites. Every rounded corner, every lowercase label, every spring-out animation is a deliberate choice — not a template applied. The handmade quality comes from specificity, not looseness.
2. **Musicians, not managers.** Commits are creative acts, not tickets. Branch names are song ideas, not sprint tasks. Copy and affordances should reflect that music is the job, not project management.
3. **Dark by default, for good reason.** Musicians work in dim rooms at night, next to audio gear with glowing screens. The UI should feel native to that environment — not a developer-default dark theme, but a workspace that earns its darkness.
4. **Speed is a feature.** Inspired by monkeytype and linear.app: keyboard-fast, instant feedback, no loading spinners where transitions will do. Snappy is part of the feel.
5. **Lowercase signals confidence.** Using lowercase in labels, buttons, and navigation reads as casual-expert, not formal-corporate. It's a personality choice that carries through every surface consistently.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best effort. No hard WCAG level committed, but: text contrast should be readable in dim environments (≥4.5:1 for body), keyboard navigation should be functional, and reduced-motion preferences should be respected.
